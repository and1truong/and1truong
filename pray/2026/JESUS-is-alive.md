# From Falling Down to Rising Again

**Key Scripture: Acts 1:1–3; Acts 9; John 15:5**

> “After His suffering, He presented Himself to them and gave many convincing proofs that He was alive. He appeared to them over a period of forty days and spoke about the kingdom of God.” — Acts 1:3

> “I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever remains in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for apart from Me you can do nothing.” — John 15:5

---

You know Paul: his journeys, churches, letters, and suffering for Christ. Two thousand years later, his ministry still builds us up.

But one part is often missed. In Jerusalem, Paul preached and debated; opposition erupted, people sought to kill him, and believers sent him to Tarsus. Luke then records that the church enjoyed peace.

Saul then almost disappears from Acts. More than a decade of his life is compressed into a few verses.

Surprisingly, that quiet season may have been the most important in shaping Paul—and us through his ministry.

To understand that silence, we must return to Saul’s question on the Damascus road: “Who are You, Lord?”

The Lord answered: **“I am Jesus.”**

Jesus—just a name. Yet before that name, all Saul’s knowledge, zeal, and noise had to fall silent so that he could truly know: Jesus had risen, He was alive, and He was speaking.

Acts 1:1–3 establishes this truth as the foundation: the risen Christ appeared over forty days, continuing to teach and prepare His disciples. Paul’s story shows that He is still doing the same today.

---

## A Man Who Knew Much About God

Before he became known as Paul, his name was Saul.

From the outside, Saul seemed very close to God. He had studied under Gamaliel, understood the Scriptures, lived by the strict demands of the law, and was deeply zealous for his faith.

Saul was not indifferent toward religion. He was so serious about it that he was willing to travel from city to city to defend what he believed was right. When he set out for Damascus, he carried letters from the high priest authorizing him to arrest the followers of Jesus and bring them back to Jerusalem.

Saul had knowledge. He had discipline. He had zeal. He also had a mission that he firmly believed came from God.

But he did not know Jesus.

This is a truth that should make us pause. A person can know many things about God and still not truly know Him. A person can be intensely active in religion and still not walk with Jesus as One who is alive.

Revival does not begin when we acquire more information about God. Revival begins when we encounter Him personally.

---

## When the Risen Christ Called Saul by Name

On the road to Damascus, a light from heaven suddenly flashed around Saul. He fell to the ground and heard a voice say:

> “Saul, Saul, why are you persecuting Me?”

Saul asked:

> “Who are You, Lord?”

The voice answered:

> “I am Jesus, whom you are persecuting.”

“Who are You, Lord?” This may be the central question of Paul’s entire life.

Saul thought he knew God. He thought he was fighting for God. But when the living Jesus met him, Saul discovered that his zeal and knowledge could not replace a genuine relationship with the Lord.

This was not merely the moment when Saul was struck down. First and foremost, it was the moment when the risen Christ took the initiative and came to meet him.

If Jesus were only a figure from the past, He could not have stopped Saul on the Damascus road. He could not have called him by name. He could not have exposed what was wrong within him or opened the way to a new life.

But Jesus is alive.

The One who had appeared to His disciples over forty days also appeared to Saul. The One who had taught and prepared the apostles continued to intervene in the life of a man who was heading in the wrong direction. Acts does not merely record what the apostles did for Jesus; it shows the risen Christ continuing to work in and through people.

The title “From Falling Down to Rising Again” does not mean that Paul was physically raised from the dead as Jesus was. Jesus alone conquered death and rose in glory. But when Saul fell before the risen Christ, his old life was exposed, and the new life of Christ began transforming him.

Our hope is not found in Saul’s fall. Our hope is found in the One standing before him—the living Jesus.

---

## He Did Not Leave Saul on the Ground

Sometimes we tell the Damascus-road story mainly as the story of God bringing a proud man down. But if we stop there, we miss its most beautiful part.

Jesus did not come to leave Saul on the ground. He came to save him and raise him up.

Saul rose but could no longer see. For three days, he neither ate nor drank; he could only wait. The man who had once been so certain of where he was going now had to let others take him by the hand and lead him.

While Saul was praying, the Lord Jesus spoke to a disciple named Ananias and sent him to Saul. Ananias was afraid because he had heard what Saul had done to the church. But the Lord said:

> “Go, for this man is My chosen instrument to carry My name before the Gentiles, their kings, and the people of Israel.” — Acts 9:15

Jesus saw a man whom Ananias could not yet see. In the eyes of many believers, Saul was still their persecutor. But Jesus saw someone whom His grace would transform.

Ananias went to Saul, placed his hands on him, and called him “Brother Saul.” He explained that Jesus Himself had sent him so that Saul might see again and be filled with the Holy Spirit. Immediately, something like scales fell from Saul’s eyes. He could see, stood up, was baptized, ate, and regained his strength. Afterward, he stayed with the disciples in Damascus.

Look at what the Lord did:

- He met Saul while Saul was heading in the wrong direction.
- He called Saul by name and confronted him with the truth.
- He sent a disciple to stand beside him.
- He restored Saul’s sight and filled him with the Holy Spirit.
- He brought the persecutor into the very community he had once harmed.
- He gave Saul a new direction and purpose.

This is the work of our living Savior. He does not merely forgive the past; He creates a new future. He does not merely correct behavior; He transforms people.

---

## Before God Worked Through Paul, He Worked in Paul

After this encounter, Saul began proclaiming that Jesus is the Son of God and the Christ. Acts 9 shows his witness growing stronger, while opposition and plots against his life also increased. The disciples helped him escape from Damascus, and later the believers sent him to Tarsus.

Scripture does not allow us to conclude that Paul was wrong to testify about Jesus immediately. Nor does it say that the church found peace simply because he left. What the text shows more clearly is that Paul’s calling was not formed in a single moment.

Galatians 1 and 2 show that many years passed between his encounter on the Damascus road and the later stages of his ministry. In Acts 11, Barnabas went to Tarsus to look for Saul and brought him to Antioch. There Saul served alongside others and helped build up the church. Then, in Acts 13, as the church worshiped, fasted, and prayed, the Holy Spirit said:

> “Set apart for Me Barnabas and Saul for the work to which I have called them.”

The church laid hands on them and sent them out.

God had called Saul in Acts 9, but He also used time, community, and the guidance of the Holy Spirit to shape him. God was not concerned only with how much Paul would accomplish. He cared about the person Paul would become.

The man who once used his authority to arrest others would later call himself a servant of Jesus Christ. The man who once put complete confidence in his religious credentials would later count them as loss compared with the surpassing worth of knowing Jesus Christ. The man who once acted according to his own certainty would later say:

> “I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.” — 1 Corinthians 3:6

Paul did not lose his abilities. His knowledge was not discarded. His zeal did not fade. But all of it was placed under the lordship of Jesus and used by the Holy Spirit.

Before God worked through Paul, He worked in Paul. Even after entering ministry, Paul continued to live by the grace and power of God.

---

## Is This Only One Interpretation of Paul’s Story?

We must be careful not to take one detail from Paul’s life and impose our subjective ideas upon it. So let us briefly consider several other biblical figures to test whether this pattern appears elsewhere in Scripture.

Moses longed to deliver his people, but God used his years in the wilderness to teach him dependence on God’s presence. Peter genuinely loved Jesus but had placed too much confidence in his own strength; the risen Christ sought him out, restored him, and entrusted His flock to him. Elijah could become exhausted even after a great victory; God met him in his weakness, cared for him, and then gave him his next assignment. The church in Ephesus had deeds, hard work, and perseverance, but Jesus called them back to their first love.

These stories are not identical, and we should not force everyone into the same pattern. Yet they confirm a shared truth: God is not looking merely for activity, ability, or enthusiasm. He wants us to belong to Him, to be encountered by Him, and to be transformed by Him.

Paul remains our focus. The other figures simply help us see that what happened on the Damascus road is not a theory constructed from one isolated case. Throughout Scripture, God cares about relationship and inward transformation before outward results.

---

## “Remain in Me”

Jesus said in John 15:5:

> “I am the vine; you are the branches. Whoever remains in Me, and I in him, bears much fruit; for apart from Me you can do nothing.”

A branch does not bear fruit by straining to prove that it is alive. It bears fruit because it remains connected to the source of life.

The Christian life is the same. We cannot replace abiding in Jesus with knowledge about Him. We cannot replace a relationship with Jesus with activity for Him. Nor can we live forever on the strength of an encounter with Him in the past.

Jesus is alive today. Therefore, we can walk with Him today.

He meets us through His Word. He speaks to us by the Holy Spirit. He uses the church and brothers and sisters like Ananias—people who obey Him and come alongside us. He reveals what must change, restores what has been broken, and leads us into the work He is doing.

Revival begins here. It does not begin primarily with a major program, an intense emotion, or a promise that we will try harder. Revival begins when Jesus is no longer merely a figure in a Bible story but the living Savior whom we know, listen to, and walk with.

---

## Where Is Jesus Meeting Us Today?

We may not meet Jesus in a blinding light as Paul did. But the One who met him is still alive, and He still takes the initiative to enter people’s lives.

Perhaps He is meeting us in the middle of a plan we are certain is right, asking whether we are truly listening to Him.

Perhaps He is meeting us in failure—not to condemn us but to free us from confidence in ourselves.

Perhaps He is using an “Ananias” to bring us healing and encouragement and to lead us back into community.

Or perhaps we have served God for many years, but our relationship with Him is slowly becoming a collection of facts, habits, and responsibilities. We still know what to say and what to do, but we no longer speak with Jesus as a Friend who is alive.

The question for us today is not merely, “What do I know about Jesus?”

It is, “Do I know Him? Am I listening to Him and walking with Him?”

Not merely, “How much am I doing for God?”

But, “What does God want to transform in me?”

Paul fell to the ground, and with him fell many of the certainties he had once held so firmly. But he was not left there. The living Jesus met him, raised him up, and gave him a new life.

This is our hope as well. Our hope does not rest on our being strong enough, gifted enough, or zealous enough. Our hope rests on the truth that Jesus Christ has risen. He is alive, He is present, and He is at work among us.

Revival begins when we stop treating Jesus as a historical figure and begin walking with Him as a living Friend.

Let us pray.
